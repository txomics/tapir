A field guide to long-read RNA-seq analysis

Author

Michael Love, Justin Landis, Beatriz Campillo, and Rachel Sharp

Published

November 18, 2026

Welcome

Welcome to TAPIR (Transcript Analysis Pipelines in R), an open-source guide for analyzing long-read RNA sequencing data in R.

While long-read sequencing technologies enable full-length isoform resolution, downstream analysis in R often requires juggling transcript-level count matrices, gene-level aggregations, and exon-level genomic coordinates. Here, we provide modular workflows guiding you from [transcript abundance tables] all the way to [biological interpretation].

Who this book is for

  • You’ve analyzed short-read RNA-seq and now have long-read data. Start with the Introduction. (Expand here, describe different usage cases)
  • Your sequencing core sent you a GTF and a count table. The section Where TAPIR fits gives an introduction to…; then pick a workflow below.
  • You build tools or teach. We design discrete, modular tools that can be used on their own or stacked with other workflows, and we welcome new ones (see Contributing and citing below).

You don’t need prior long-read experience. Some familiarity with R and the tidyverse will help.

How to use this book

  • Part I: Foundations introduces the concepts.
  • Part II: Workflows are independent, hands-on analyses of real datasets.

Workflows at a glance

Workflow The question Dataset Key packages
Exploring a long-read transcriptome What did we detect, and how much of it is novel? Gandal et al., developing human brain (PacBio Iso-Seq, TALON) readr, dplyr, plyxp
Gene- and transcript-level testing Which genes change in total, and which change their isoform mix? Glinos et al., PTBP1 knockdown (ONT, GTEx v9) tximeta, DESeq2, satuRn, plyxp, Glimma
Splicing events, step by step How do I go from a DTU result to a specific splicing event? Small simulated dataset plyxp, splicelogic
Splicing events in real data Which exons does PTBP1 regulate, and what sequence surrounds them? PTBP1 knockdown splicelogic, plyranges, Biostrings, SPLain
Allele-specific expression (coming soon) Is one parental copy of a gene expressed more than the other? TBD GATK, samtools, …

Featured tools

plyxp: tidy verbs (mutate(), filter(), group_by(), summarize()) for SummarizedExperiment objects. (Succinct description of the benefit/use case of plyxp) Grouping transcripts by gene to compute isoform proportions becomes a single readable step. Landis & Love (2026), doi:10.64898/2026.05.06.721669

splicelogic: turns any two sets of transcripts into discrete splicing events. It reports skipped and included exons, mutually exclusive exons, retained introns, alternative 5′/3′ splice sites, and alternative transcription start/end sites. The two sets can come from the results of any DTU method or from a simple split such as novel vs. annotated isoforms. Results come back as genomic ranges, ready for plyranges and Biostrings. (Is this a good enough description? Update as needed) Campillo & Love (2026), doi:10.64898/2026.10.30.762958

Getting started

You’ll need R ≥ 4.6 and Bioconductor ≥ 3.23.

if (!requireNamespace("BiocManager", quietly = TRUE))
  install.packages("BiocManager")

BiocManager::install(c(
  # Containers and tidy grammar
  "SummarizedExperiment", "GenomicRanges", "GenomicFeatures",
  "plyxp", "plyranges", "dplyr", "tidyr", "readr", "stringr", "tibble", "here",

  # Import, annotation and statistics
  "tximeta", "DESeq2", "satuRn", "limma", "org.Hs.eg.db", "AnnotationDbi",

  # Splicing events, sequences and visualization
  "splicelogic", "Biostrings", "BSgenome.Hsapiens.UCSC.hg38",
  "Glimma", "htmlwidgets", "shiny"
))

Some workflows also use the interactive SPLain app:

git clone https://github.com/beamimc/SPLain.git

Contributing and citing

If TAPIR or its featured tools help your work, please cite the papers listed under Featured tools.
